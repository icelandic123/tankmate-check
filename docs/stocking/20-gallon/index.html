20-Gallon Tank Stocking Ideas

A 20-gallon (76 L) tank can hold 3 different community setups that all pass our compatibility checks. Each plan lists a centerpiece, a school, and a bottom or cleanup crew, with the shared temperature and pH that suit every species in it.

Plan 1: Dwarf Gourami + Cardinal Tetra + Cockatoo Dwarf Cichlid + Mystery Snail

  • 1 × Dwarf Gourami (centerpiece): Males are territorial toward other gouramis and bettas. Buy from a careful source: the species is prone to dwarf gourami iridovirus.
  • 6 × Cardinal Tetra (school): Prefers warmer, softer water than the neon tetra it is often confused with.
  • 1 × Cockatoo Dwarf Cichlid (bottom): Keep one male with one or more females; males are territorial.
  • 1 × Mystery Snail (cleanup crew): Long feelers attract fin nippers. Needs hard water for a healthy shell.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 77–82 °F (25–28 °C), pH 7–7.

Plan 2: Platy + Harlequin Rasbora + Nerite Snail

  • 3 × Platy (centerpiece): One of the hardiest community fish; prefers harder water.
  • 8 × Harlequin Rasbora (school): A tight, calm shoaler that suits community tanks well.
  • 1 × Nerite Snail (cleanup crew): An excellent algae eater that does not reproduce in fresh water, though it lays hard white eggs.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 72–79 °F (22–26 °C), pH 7–7.5.

Plan 3: Guppy + Ember Tetra + Bronze Corydoras + Mystery Snail

  • 3 × Guppy (centerpiece): Keep more females than males (about 2 to 3 per male) so no single female is harassed. Breeds constantly.
  • 8 × Ember Tetra (school): Tiny and timid; shows its best color in a planted tank with dim spots.
  • 6 × Bronze Corydoras (bottom): Needs sand or smooth gravel to protect its barbels, and sinking food that reaches the bottom.
  • 1 × Mystery Snail (cleanup crew): Long feelers attract fin nippers. Needs hard water for a healthy shell.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 73–79 °F (23–26 °C), pH 7–7.

Before you buy

  • Cycle the tank first. Add fish over several weeks, starting with the hardiest group, so the filter bacteria keep up.
  • The counts are the minimum group sizes. In a 20-gallon tank, stick close to them rather than adding extra species.
  • Quarantine new fish if you can, and watch the first two weeks closely.
