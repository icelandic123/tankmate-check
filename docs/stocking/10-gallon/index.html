10-Gallon Tank Stocking Ideas

A 10-gallon (38 L) tank can hold 3 different community setups that all pass our compatibility checks. Each plan lists a centerpiece, a school, and a bottom or cleanup crew, with the shared temperature and pH that suit every species in it.

Plan 1: Guppy + Ember Tetra + Mystery Snail

  • 3 × Guppy (centerpiece): Keep more females than males (about 2 to 3 per male) so no single female is harassed. Breeds constantly.
  • 8 × Ember Tetra (school): Tiny and timid; shows its best color in a planted tank with dim spots.
  • 1 × Mystery Snail (cleanup crew): Long feelers attract fin nippers. Needs hard water for a healthy shell.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 73–82 °F (23–28 °C), pH 7–7.

Plan 2: Betta Fish + Neon Tetra + Nerite Snail

  • 1 × Betta Fish (centerpiece): Males must be kept alone: two males fight, often to the death. Bettas may flare at or attack bright, long-finned fish they mistake for rivals.
  • 6 × Neon Tetra (school): Sensitive to swings in water quality; add only to a fully cycled, stable tank.
  • 1 × Nerite Snail (cleanup crew): An excellent algae eater that does not reproduce in fresh water, though it lays hard white eggs.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 75–79 °F (24–26 °C), pH 7–7.

Plan 3: Endler's Livebearer + Celestial Pearl Danio + Mystery Snail

  • 3 × Endler's Livebearer (centerpiece): Hybridizes readily with guppies; keep them apart if you want pure lines.
  • 6 × Celestial Pearl Danio (school): Shy; prefers cooler water than most tropical community fish.
  • 1 × Mystery Snail (cleanup crew): Long feelers attract fin nippers. Needs hard water for a healthy shell.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 72–77 °F (22–25 °C), pH 7–7.5.

Before you buy

  • Cycle the tank first. Add fish over several weeks, starting with the hardiest group, so the filter bacteria keep up.
  • The counts are the minimum group sizes. In a 10-gallon tank, stick close to them rather than adding extra species.
  • Quarantine new fish if you can, and watch the first two weeks closely.
