40-Gallon Tank Stocking Ideas

A 40-gallon (151 L) tank can hold 3 different community setups that all pass our compatibility checks. Each plan lists a centerpiece, a school, and a bottom or cleanup crew, with the shared temperature and pH that suit every species in it.

Plan 1: Molly + Tiger Barb + Bristlenose Pleco + Nerite Snail

  • 3–7 × Molly (centerpiece): Prefers hard, alkaline water; struggles in soft, acidic tanks.
  • 6–10 × Tiger Barb (school): The classic fin nipper. In groups of 6 or more they chase each other more and tankmates less.
  • 1 × Bristlenose Pleco (bottom): Stays small for a pleco. Males defend caves from other plecos. Needs driftwood and vegetables.
  • 1 × Nerite Snail (cleanup crew): An excellent algae eater that does not reproduce in fresh water, though it lays hard white eggs.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 75–81 °F (24–27 °C), pH 7–7.5.

Plan 2: Dwarf Gourami + Cardinal Tetra + Kribensis + Mystery Snail

  • 1 × Dwarf Gourami (centerpiece): Males are territorial toward other gouramis and bettas. Buy from a careful source: the species is prone to dwarf gourami iridovirus.
  • 6–10 × Cardinal Tetra (school): Prefers warmer, softer water than the neon tetra it is often confused with.
  • 1 × Kribensis (bottom): Peaceful most of the time, but a breeding pair defends its cave hard.
  • 1 × Mystery Snail (cleanup crew): Long feelers attract fin nippers. Needs hard water for a healthy shell.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 77–81 °F (25–27 °C), pH 7–7.

Plan 3: Platy + Harlequin Rasbora + Bronze Corydoras + Mystery Snail

  • 3–7 × Platy (centerpiece): One of the hardiest community fish; prefers harder water.
  • 8–12 × Harlequin Rasbora (school): A tight, calm shoaler that suits community tanks well.
  • 6–10 × Bronze Corydoras (bottom): Needs sand or smooth gravel to protect its barbels, and sinking food that reaches the bottom.
  • 1 × Mystery Snail (cleanup crew): Long feelers attract fin nippers. Needs hard water for a healthy shell.

Every pair in this plan checks out as compatible. Shared water: 72–79 °F (22–26 °C), pH 7–7.5.

Before you buy

  • Cycle the tank first. Add fish over several weeks, starting with the hardiest group, so the filter bacteria keep up.
  • The counts are the minimum group sizes. In a 40-gallon tank, stick close to them rather than adding extra species.
  • Quarantine new fish if you can, and watch the first two weeks closely.
