How the checks work

Every species has a profile: adult size, minimum tank, temperature range, pH range, temperament, group size, swimming level, diet, and flags for fin nipping, long fins, and eating shrimp or snails. For each pair, Tankmate Check checks:

  • Water: whether their temperature and pH ranges overlap, and by how much.
  • Predation: whether one grows big enough to swallow the other, and whether it eats shrimp or snails.
  • Fins: whether a known fin nipper would share a tank with long-finned or slow fish.
  • Temperament and territory: aggressive fish with peaceful ones, and territorial fish that claim the same part of the tank.
  • Feeding: slow eaters that would be out-competed.

Some well-known pairs, like bettas with gouramis, get a hand-written verdict where the general rules are too blunt. When the rules are unsure, they say "with caution" rather than "yes".

Fish are individuals. A peaceful species can still produce a bully, and a semi-aggressive one can be fine in a large, planted tank. Use the verdicts as a starting point, quarantine new arrivals, and watch new tankmates closely for the first weeks.

Found a verdict you disagree with? That feedback is what improves the data.
