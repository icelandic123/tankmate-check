## Overview
The African dwarf frog is a small, fully aquatic frog from central and western Africa. It spends its life in the water and does not need a land area, though it comes to the surface to breathe. These frogs are friendly and easy to keep, and they are a good choice for a beginner who wants something different. Adults reach about 2.4 in (6 cm).

African dwarf frogs are social and do best in a group of two or more. They are nearly blind and find food mostly by touch, so they are slow eaters. Fast tankmates often take their food before the frogs find it.

## Tank setup
A 10 gallon (38 L) tank is the minimum for a pair. Keep the water at 72–79 °F (22–26 °C) with a pH of 6.5–7.8. Use fine sand or smooth gravel, and add plants and hiding places for cover. Choose gentle filtration with an intake guard, since the frogs can be pulled into strong flow. A tight-fitting lid helps, because these frogs can climb out of an open tank.

## Feeding
African dwarf frogs are carnivores. Offer sinking pellets made for frogs or small fish, along with frozen or live foods such as bloodworms and brine shrimp a few times a week. Drop food close to the frogs and feed slowly so they have time to find it. Feed small amounts every other day.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them with fast or aggressive fish that eat their food before they can find it.
- Keeping a single frog, when these social animals do best in a group.
- Dropping food from above into the middle of the tank, where it sinks away from the frogs.
- Using a filter intake with no guard, which can draw in and injure the frogs.
