## Overview
The glass catfish is a small, nearly transparent catfish from rivers in Thailand. Adults reach about 3 inches (8 cm). The most useful fact for a beginner is that it must be kept in a group of at least six. Its body is so clear that you can see the skeleton and internal organs, which is one of its main attractions. Glass catfish are very shy, so the group needs a calm setting to feel at home.

In the wild they live in schools in midwater. In a home tank they do best in a quiet, planted aquarium with dim light and floating plants, away from boisterous fish.

## Tank setup
A group of six needs at least 30 gallons (114 L). Keep the water at 75–81 °F (24–27 °C) and the pH between 6.5 and 7.5. Use subdued lighting, a dense planted area, and some floating plants, while leaving open swimming space in the middle. Smooth sand or fine gravel works well on the bottom, and a gentle filter with low current suits them. Keep the tank mature and stable before adding them.

## Feeding
Glass catfish are omnivores that feed on small particles in midwater and near the surface. Offer a quality flake or micro pellet, and add frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, or bloodworms a few times a week. Feed small amounts once or twice a day, since they can be slow to compete with faster fish at feeding time.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six, which leaves them hiding and declining.
- Placing them with loud, fast, or boisterous fish that keep them stressed.
- Using bright lighting and open water with no plant cover.
- Adding them to a new tank before the water chemistry has settled.
