## Overview
Mollies are livebearing fish from Mexico and Central America, where they live in both fresh and brackish coastal waters. Their most useful trait for a beginner is that they give birth to free-swimming young and do well in hard, alkaline water, which many tap supplies provide. Adults reach about 4 in (10 cm), and they come in many color and fin types.

Mollies are peaceful and make good community fish with other calm, mid-sized tankmates. Keep a group with more females than males, since males can be persistent and a single female may be harassed. They breed readily, so plan for fry.

## Tank setup
A tank of at least 30 gallons (114 L) suits a small group of mollies. Keep the water at 75–82 °F (24–28 °C) with a pH of 7.0–8.5, and use hard water. A planted tank with open swimming space works well, and floating plants give pregnant females and fry places to hide. Use a gentle filter with an intake guard so young fish are not drawn in.

## Feeding
Mollies are omnivores and benefit from a varied diet. Offer a quality flake or pellet that includes plant matter, and add blanched spinach, zucchini, or algae wafers a few times a week. Include some protein, such as brine shrimp or bloodworms, once or twice a week. Feed small portions that the fish finish within a few minutes.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them in soft, acidic water, which harms their health and can cause problems over time.
- Keeping too few females for the number of males, which leaves the females harassed and stressed.
- Feeding only flakes with no plant matter, which does not suit their omnivorous diet.
- Leaving no shaded or planted areas for pregnant females and fry to hide from the adults.
