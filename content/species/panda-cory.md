## Overview
The panda cory is a small, social catfish from Peru, in South America, where it lives in slow-moving rivers and streams. The most useful fact for a beginner is that it must be kept in a group of at least six. A lone cory is stressed and rarely shows its natural behavior. Adults reach about 2 inches (5 cm), with a pale body and dark patches that give it the panda look.

It spends most of its time on the bottom, sifting through the substrate as a busy, friendly group. It prefers water a little cooler than many tropical fish, so it suits community tanks that are kept at moderate temperatures.

## Tank setup
A group of six needs at least 15 gallons (57 L). Keep the water at 68–77 °F (20–25 °C) and the pH between 6.0 and 7.5. Use sand or smooth gravel, since sharp substrate can damage the delicate barbels around the mouth. Add hiding places such as caves or leaf litter, and plant the back of the tank. Use a gentle filter with low current, and keep the water well oxygenated.

## Feeding
Panda corys are omnivores that feed mainly from the bottom. Offer sinking pellets, wafers, or tablets that reach the substrate, and add small amounts of frozen or live foods such as bloodworms or brine shrimp a few times a week. Feed once a day, only as much as the group finishes within a few minutes, since leftovers can rot in the substrate.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six, which leaves them hidden and unsettled.
- Relying only on surface flakes, so the bottom-feeding group goes hungry.
- Using sharp gravel that can damage their barbels.
- Keeping the water above about 77 °F (25 °C) for long periods.
