## Overview
The rummy-nose tetra is a schooling fish from the Amazon basin of South America. Its most recognizable feature is a bright red head set against a pale body with a dark stripe, and the color is most vivid when the fish is kept in clean, well-maintained water. It is a peaceful fish that suits a community tank as long as it is kept in a school of eight or more.

Rummy-nose tetras move together in tight, coordinated groups, and a fish kept alone tends to become nervous and pale. The red nose is also a useful warning sign: when it looks washed out, check the water quality before assuming the fish itself is sick.

## Tank setup
A school of eight needs a tank of at least 20 gallons (76 L). Keep the water at 75–82 °F (24–28 °C) with a pH of 5.5–7.0, and keep it stable and clean. Plant the back and sides of the tank, leave an open swimming area in front, and choose a dark substrate so the red head stands out. Gentle to moderate flow suits them, and a lid helps keep them from jumping.

## Feeding
Rummy-nose tetras are omnivores and usually eat readily. A good flake or small pellet food should make up the base of the diet, with frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp and bloodworms offered a few times a week. Feed small amounts once or twice a day, and only as much as the fish finish within a couple of minutes.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than eight, which leaves the school scattered and the fish timid.
- Letting nitrate build up between water changes, which fades the red nose and stresses the fish.
- Adding them to a tank that has not finished cycling.
- Using bright lighting with no plants or cover, which keeps the school on edge and pale.
