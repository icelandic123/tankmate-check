## Overview
The Amano shrimp is a translucent, algae-eating shrimp from Asia. It is larger than the cherry shrimp and is well known as a dependable grazer that helps keep algae under control. It is a good choice for a beginner who wants a useful, low-maintenance invertebrate in a planted tank.

Amano shrimp are peaceful and do best in a group of at least three. They spend most of their time on the bottom and on plants, grazing. Keep in mind that they do not breed in fresh water, so you should not expect a population of babies.

## Tank setup
A group needs a tank of at least 10 gallons (38 L). Keep the water between 64 and 82 °F (18–28 °C) with a pH of 6.0 to 7.8. Provide plants and wood or stones for grazing and hiding, and keep the water clean and stable. Gentle flow is best, and standard lighting is fine. Choose a substrate that suits your plants, since the shrimp will forage over it.

## Feeding
Amano shrimp are omnivores that graze on algae and biofilm. Keep algae growing in the tank, and supplement with blanched vegetables such as zucchini or spinach, along with sinking shrimp pellets. Remove uneaten food so the water stays clean.

## Common mistakes
- Relying on algae alone, which may not be enough for a group of shrimp, so offer some supplementary food.
- Using copper-based medicines, which are toxic to shrimp and invertebrates.
- Expecting babies in fresh water, since this species does not breed there.
- Keeping them with cichlids or other fish that eat shrimp, which will prey on the group.
