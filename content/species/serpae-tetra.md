## Overview
Serpae tetras are persistent fin nippers, so keep at least six together to spread the nipping across the group. They are schooling fish from tropical South America, growing to a typical adult length of about 1.8 in (4.5 cm). Their red body makes them an eye-catching addition to a community tank.

They are usually easy to keep and eat most standard foods. Their active, boisterous nature suits a tank with open swimming room, and they do best with tankmates that have short, sturdy fins.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 20 gallons (76 L) for a group of six. Use a planted tank with plants along the back and sides, plus open swimming lanes in the middle. Driftwood and a sand or fine-gravel bottom suit them, and moderate lighting with some shade helps show their color. Keep the water at 72–81 °F (22–27 °C) with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5. Avoid housing them with long-finned fish such as bettas, since the nipping can ruin those fins.

## Feeding
Serpae tetras are omnivores. A quality flake or small pellet makes a good daily staple, offered in small amounts that are gone within a few minutes. Add frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, or bloodworms a few times a week, and include some blanched vegetables or algae-based foods for variety. Because they are active and eager eaters, watch for overfeeding, which pollutes the water.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six, which concentrates the nipping on the few fish present.
- Pairing them with long-finned tankmates such as bettas, angelfish, or fancy goldfish.
- Choosing a tank smaller than 20 gallons (76 L) for a full group.
- Assuming they are tough enough to skip regular water changes.
