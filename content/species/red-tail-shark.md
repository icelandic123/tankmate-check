## Overview
The red tail shark is not a true shark. It is a black-bodied cyprinid from Southeast Asia, named for its bright red-orange tail. It is a striking bottom fish that many hobbyists keep as a centerpiece in a larger tank.

Red tail sharks are territorial, and they chase other bottom dwellers and fish that look similar to them. Keep only one per tank. They grow to about 6 inches (15 cm) and are best suited to a beginner who has room for a large tank and can plan the tankmates carefully.

## Tank setup
A single red tail shark needs a tank of at least 55 gallons (208 L). Keep the water between 72 and 81 °F (22–27 °C) with a pH of 6.5 to 7.5. Provide open floor space for swimming, along with rocks, driftwood, and caves for shelter. Plant the tank with sturdy plants along the back and sides. Moderate flow and standard lighting suit them well, and a lid helps keep the tank secure.

## Feeding
Red tail sharks are omnivores. Offer sinking pellets or wafers, some vegetable matter, and occasional frozen foods such as bloodworms. Feed once or twice a day in moderate portions so that food is not left to decay on the bottom.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping more than one, which leads to chasing and fighting over territory.
- Stocking with other bottom dwellers or fish with a similar shape and color, which become targets.
- Choosing a tank smaller than 55 gallons, which leaves the fish cramped and aggressive.
- Adding shrimp, which the red tail shark may eat.
