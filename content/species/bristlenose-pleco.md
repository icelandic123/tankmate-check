## Overview
The bristlenose pleco is a South American armored catfish from the Amazon basin and nearby rivers. It is a sturdy algae eater, and it is the pleco most often recommended for home aquariums because it stays small for its group. Adults reach about 4.7 in (12 cm). Males grow fleshy bristles around the snout, which give the fish its name.

Bristlenose plecos are peaceful but territorial. Males defend caves from other plecos, so a single fish or one pair in a well-decorated tank is the usual arrangement. They are mostly active at night and spend much of the day resting in a hiding place.

## Tank setup
A 30 gallon (114 L) tank is the minimum, and a larger tank makes room for multiple hides. Keep the water at 70–81 °F (21–27 °C) with a pH of 6.5–7.5. Provide driftwood, which they rasp for fiber, along with caves or hollow decor so each fish has its own shelter. Use smooth sand or fine gravel, and keep the water well oxygenated with good flow. A secure lid is a sensible precaution.

## Feeding
Bristlenose plecos are herbivores. Offer sinking algae wafers or blanched vegetables such as zucchini, cucumber, or spinach, and keep driftwood and natural biofilm in the tank for grazing. They feed mostly at night, so food left in the evening is often eaten after the lights go off. Remove uneaten vegetables within a day or two.

## Common mistakes
- Relying on algae wafers alone, when they also need fresh vegetables and wood in the diet.
- Keeping two males together, which leads to fights over caves.
- Housing them in a tank smaller than 30 gallons, which limits space and shelter.
- Failing to provide enough caves, so the fish are stressed, hide all day, and are rarely seen.
