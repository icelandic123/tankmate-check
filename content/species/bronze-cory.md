## Overview
The bronze corydoras is a small armored catfish from slow-moving rivers, streams, and ponds in northern South America and Trinidad. It is one of the easiest community catfish for a beginner, and it is a steady, friendly bottom dweller that spends most of its day sifting the substrate. Its bronze-colored body and small barbels near the mouth are easy to recognize, and adults reach about 2.6 in (6.5 cm).

Bronze corydoras are social and do best in a group of six or more. They become stressed when kept alone, and a school will be more active and easier to observe. They will come to the surface now and then to gulp air, which is normal for this group of catfish.

## Tank setup
A 20 gallon (76 L) tank is a sensible minimum for a group of six. Keep the water at 72–79 °F (22–26 °C) with a pH of 6.0–8.0. Use sand or smooth gravel, since sharp substrate can injure the barbels. Provide open floor space, plants, and some driftwood or caves for shelter. Moderate flow and a lid are fine, but keep the bottom clean so the fish have a safe place to forage.

## Feeding
Bronze corydoras are omnivores that scavenge the bottom. Offer quality sinking pellets or wafers daily, and add frozen or live foods such as bloodworms or brine shrimp a few times a week. Make sure the food reaches the bottom before faster fish take it, and remove any leftovers before they decay.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six, which leaves the fish stressed and withdrawn.
- Using sharp gravel that cuts or wears down the delicate barbels.
- Relying on leftovers from other fish, when the corydoras need sinking food of their own.
- Overlooking the bottom, letting waste collect where the fish feed and foul the water.
