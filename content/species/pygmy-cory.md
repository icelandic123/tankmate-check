## Overview
The pygmy cory is one of the smallest corydoras catfish, reaching about 1 inch (2.5 cm) as an adult. It comes from South America. Unlike most corydoras, which spend their time on the bottom, pygmy corys often swim in open midwater as a group.

These are peaceful, social fish that need a school of at least eight to feel secure. Their small size and calm temperament make them a good match for a planted community tank of small fish. Watch that they get enough food, since they are easily outcompeted at feeding time by faster fish.

## Tank setup
A group needs a tank of at least 10 gallons (38 L). Keep the water between 72 and 79 °F (22–26 °C) with a pH of 6.0 to 7.5. Leave clear swimming space in the middle of the tank and add plants along the back and sides. Use gentle flow and a fine sand or smooth gravel bottom, as with other corydoras. Standard lighting is fine.

## Feeding
Pygmy corys are omnivores. Offer sinking micro pellets or a sinking wafer once or twice a day, since food needs to reach the bottom and midwater. Add small frozen foods such as brine shrimp or micro bloodworms a few times a week.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than eight, which leaves them stressed and less active.
- Assuming they stay on the bottom like other corydoras and offering only sinking food where they cannot reach it.
- Using coarse or sharp gravel, which can injure their delicate barbels.
- Letting the water run warm, above about 79 °F (26 °C), which is the top of their comfortable range.
