## Overview
Zebra danios are hardy and easy to keep, but they need a group of at least six and plenty of open swimming room. They are small, fast schooling fish from the rivers of northern India and neighboring South Asia, growing to a typical adult length of about 2 in (5 cm). Their horizontal blue and gold stripes and constant motion make them a lively presence in the upper water.

They are peaceful with most fish of similar size, although their speed and enthusiasm can stress slow, long-finned tankmates. They spend most of their time in the upper and middle water.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 15 gallons (57 L) for a group of six, and a longer tank is better because these fish swim constantly. Leave open water in the middle and add plants along the back and sides. Any substrate works, so choose sand or gravel for ease of cleaning. Moderate flow suits them well. Use a tight lid, because they are strong jumpers. Keep the water at 64–79 °F (18–26 °C) with a pH between 6.5 and 8.0.

## Feeding
Zebra danios are omnivores and will eat almost any food offered. A quality flake or micro pellet works as a daily base, given in small portions they clear within a few minutes. Add frozen or live foods such as daphnia, brine shrimp, or bloodworms a few times a week, and offer some vegetable matter for balance. They feed near the top of the tank, and they will always act hungry, so avoid overfeeding.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six, which makes them nip long, trailing fins more often.
- Pairing them with bettas, angelfish, or other slow, long-finned fish.
- Leaving the lid off, which lets these active jumpers escape.
- Overfeeding because they are eager eaters that always look hungry.
