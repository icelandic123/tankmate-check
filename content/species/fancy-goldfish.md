## Overview
Fancy goldfish are domesticated carp, first bred from wild carp in China and refined over many centuries. They need cool water and a larger tank than most people expect, so plan for that before buying. They come in many body shapes and fin types, and they are one of the most common pet fish in the world.

Goldfish are social and do best in a group of two or more. Their slow, flowing fins and deep bodies mean they cannot keep up with fast tankmates, and they produce a lot of waste, so filtration is important. A peaceful pair in a well-filtered tank is a rewarding setup.

## Tank setup
A tank of at least 30 gallons (114 L) is recommended for a pair. Keep the water at 64–75 °F (18–24 °C) with a pH of 7.0–8.0. Use smooth gravel or sand with no sharp edges, and avoid small decor that can be swallowed. Strong filtration and regular water changes are necessary, since goldfish produce heavy waste. Choose sturdy or artificial plants, because goldfish often uproot soft plants.

## Feeding
Fancy goldfish are omnivores. Offer a quality sinking or slow-sinking pellet formulated for goldfish, and add blanched vegetables such as peas or zucchini a few times a week. Feed only what the fish finish within a couple of minutes, once or twice a day. Overfeeding causes waste to build up quickly and can lead to bloating.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them in a small bowl or underfiltered tank, since goldfish produce a lot of waste.
- Keeping them with fast or aggressive tankmates that outcompete them for food.
- Keeping them in tropical temperatures, which goes against their need for cool water.
- Overfeeding, which fouls the water and can cause digestive problems.
