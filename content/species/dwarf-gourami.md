## Overview
The dwarf gourami is a small labyrinth fish from slow-moving, heavily planted waters in South Asia. Dwarf gouramis are colorful and calm, which makes them a good fit for most community tanks, but males can be territorial with other fish, so pairing them carefully matters. Like other gouramis, they have a labyrinth organ that lets them gulp air at the surface, so they need access to the top of the tank.

Males show deeper orange and blue coloring, and they become more territorial as they mature. Females are duller and usually less aggressive. A single male with a female or two in a larger tank, or one fish alone in a smaller one, is the usual arrangement.

## Tank setup
A tank of at least 15 gallons (57 L) suits a single dwarf gourami or one male with a female or two. Keep the water at 77–82 °F (25–28 °C) with a pH of 6.0–7.5. Plant the tank heavily with both rooted and floating plants, and include hiding places. Keep the water flow gentle, since these fish prefer calm water, and leave the surface open enough for them to reach air.

## Feeding
Dwarf gouramis are omnivores. Offer a varied diet of quality flake or small pellets, with frozen or live foods such as bloodworms, daphnia, and brine shrimp a few times a week. Blanched vegetables or algae wafers add plant matter. Feed small portions once or twice a day, and remove leftovers promptly.

## Common mistakes
- Housing two males together, or pairing a male with a betta, which leads to fighting over territory.
- Buying from a source that cannot vouch for its fish, since the species is prone to dwarf gourami iridovirus.
- Setting up a tank with strong current, which these calm fish find stressful.
- Letting water quality slip, since gouramis handle poor conditions badly.
