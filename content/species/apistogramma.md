## Overview
The cockatoo dwarf cichlid is a small South American cichlid from the Amazon basin. Keep one male with one or more females, since males are territorial and will fight with each other. Males have a long, trailing dorsal fin with a distinctive crest, while females are smaller and show more yellow and orange coloring. Adults reach about 3 in (8 cm).

These fish are shy but interesting to watch, and they tend to hold a small territory around a cave or patch of the bottom. Pairs guard their eggs and fry closely, and females typically lay their eggs in a cave or sheltered spot.

## Tank setup
A 20 gallon (76 L) tank is the minimum for one male with a few females. Keep the water at 75–84 °F (24–29 °C) with a pH of 5.0–7.5. Use fine sand or smooth gravel, and provide several caves or clay pots so each female has her own shelter. Plants and decor that break up sightlines help the fish feel secure. A moderate filter with gentle flow suits them.

## Feeding
Cockatoo dwarf cichlids are carnivores. Offer quality sinking pellets or wafers, along with frozen or live foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, and daphnia. Feed small amounts once or twice a day, and make sure food reaches the bottom, where these fish feed.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping two males together, which leads to aggression and stress for the fish.
- Failing to provide enough caves, so females have no place to lay eggs or retreat.
- Housing them with fast, boisterous fish that chase them away from the bottom.
- Keeping them in very hard, alkaline water, since they do better in softer conditions.
