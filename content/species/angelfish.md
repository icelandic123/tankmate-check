## Overview
Adult angelfish need a tall tank, and they will eat fish small enough to fit in their mouths, including neon tetras. They are graceful, flat-bodied cichlids from the Amazon basin of South America, growing to a typical adult length of about 6 in (15 cm). Their long, triangular fins make them one of the most recognizable community fish.

Angelfish are semi-aggressive and can pair up and guard a territory. They are usually calm with fish too large to swallow, but they do best with tankmates chosen carefully for size and temperament.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 55 gallons (208 L), and choose a tall tank because angelfish need vertical room. Use tall plants, driftwood, and broad-leaved plants that give cover while leaving open water in the middle. Fine sand or smooth gravel works well. Moderate flow and subdued lighting suit them. Keep the water at 75–86 °F (24–30 °C) with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5. Avoid fin nippers, and avoid fish small enough for the angelfish to eat.

## Feeding
Angelfish are omnivores. A quality flake or pellet makes a good staple, offered in small amounts once or twice a day. Add frozen or live foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, or daphnia a few times a week, and offer some vegetable matter for balance. Watch for overfeeding, which fouls the water.

## Common mistakes
- Starting with a tank smaller than 55 gallons (208 L), or one too short for their height.
- Housing them with small fish like neon tetras that an adult angelfish will eat.
- Keeping them with fin nippers such as tiger barbs, which harass their long fins.
- Choosing tankmates by looks alone instead of by size and temperament.
