## Overview
The cardinal tetra is a small schooling fish from the Orinoco and Rio Negro river basins of South America, where it lives in soft, slightly acidic blackwater and clearwater streams. It is one of the most common community fish in the hobby and a good choice for a stable, established tank. Its red coloring extends farther back than a neon tetra's, which makes the two easy to tell apart.

Cardinal tetras are peaceful and do best in a group of six or more. A school that stays together swims with more confidence and shows its color far better than a few fish kept alone. They spend most of their time in the middle of the water column and rarely bother tankmates of a similar size.

## Tank setup
Plan on a tank of at least 20 gallons (76 L) for a group of six. Keep the water at 73–84 °F (23–29 °C) with a pH of 4.5–7.0, and use soft water where you can. A dark substrate and plants along the back and sides help the colors show. Leave open swimming space in the middle of the tank, and float a few plants and keep the lighting moderate so the fish feel secure. A lid is a good idea, since tetras can jump when startled.

## Feeding
Cardinal tetras are omnivores. Offer a quality flake or micro pellet every day, and add frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, or bloodworms a few times a week. Feed only what the fish finish within about two minutes, and remove any uneaten food so it does not foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six, which leaves the fish shy and stressed.
- Adding them to a new tank before it has finished cycling, since they do poorly when ammonia or nitrite spikes.
- Keeping them in hard, alkaline water, which goes against the soft, slightly acidic conditions they prefer.
- Overfeeding, which clouds the water and makes the fish's color look dull.
