## Overview
The mystery snail is a large freshwater apple snail from warm waters in the Americas. Mystery snails are peaceful cleaners, but they need hard water for healthy shells. They eat algae, biofilm, and leftover food, which makes them a useful addition to many community tanks. Adults reach about 2 in (5 cm), and they come in a range of shell colors.

A mystery snail moves slowly across glass and plants, and it can be fun to watch. It extends a breathing siphon to the surface and has two long, tentacle-like feelers that it uses to sense its surroundings.

## Tank setup
A 5 gallon (19 L) tank is the smallest practical size for one snail, but a 10 gallon (38 L) tank or larger gives more room and steadier water conditions. Keep the water at 68–82 °F (20–28 °C) with a pH of 7.0–8.0, and use hard water. Live plants, smooth gravel, and floating plants all suit them. Place a secure lid on the tank, because snails can crawl up the glass and escape.

## Feeding
Mystery snails are omnivores. Most of their diet comes from algae and biofilm, but they benefit from extra food. Offer blanched vegetables such as zucchini or spinach, and provide a calcium source such as cuttlebone or a calcium-rich food to support the shell. Remove uneaten vegetables within a day or two so they do not foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them with fin nippers or puffers, which pick at the snail's long feelers.
- Using copper-based medications, which are toxic to snails and other invertebrates.
- Keeping them in soft, acidic water, which can lead to thin or eroded shells.
- Leaving the tank without a lid, which allows the snail to climb out and escape.
