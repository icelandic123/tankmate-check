## Overview
Endler's livebearers readily breed with guppies, so keep them apart if you want pure lines. They are tiny, colorful fish from a small coastal area of northern Venezuela, growing to a typical adult length of about 1 in (2.5 cm). They are close relatives of guppies and just as easy to keep, and a group of three or more shows their natural behavior best.

They are peaceful, active fish that stay in the upper water and breed readily. Their small size and bright color suit a planted community tank.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 10 gallons (38 L) for a group of three. Use a planted tank with fine-leaved plants and open swimming space near the top. Floating plants give cover and help newborn fry survive. Use gentle to moderate flow and standard lighting. Keep the water at 72–82 °F (22–28 °C) with a pH between 7.0 and 8.5, which is slightly alkaline. A lid is optional.

## Feeding
Endler's livebearers are omnivores. Feed a quality flake or micro pellet once or twice a day in small amounts, and add frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, or bloodworms a few times a week. They also graze algae and some plant matter, so a planted tank offers extra food. Avoid overfeeding, since this fouls the water in a small tank.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing them with guppies, which produces hybrids and blurs the pure line.
- Keeping only one or two, rather than a group of three or more.
- Not planning for fry, since they breed readily and the babies need cover to survive.
- Overfeeding, which pollutes the water in a small tank.
