## Overview
The guppy is one of the easiest freshwater fish to keep and a good first choice for a beginner. It comes from slow streams and ponds in northern South America and nearby islands. Males are small and show bright colors and long, flowing fins, while females are larger and plainer.

Guppies are livebearers, so females give birth to free-swimming young rather than laying eggs. They are active, peaceful fish that do best in a group, and they breed readily in a community tank.

## Tank setup
A group of guppies needs at least 10 gallons (38 L). Keep the water between 72 and 82 °F (22–28 °C) with a pH of 6.8 to 7.8. Live or silk plants, floating plants, and some open swimming space suit them well. Use a gentle filter flow, since guppies are not strong swimmers in fast water. A plain or fine substrate works fine, and standard lighting is enough.

## Feeding
Guppies are omnivores. Offer a quality flake or small pellet food once or twice a day in small amounts that the fish finish within a couple of minutes. Add some frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia, and include plant matter like spirulina flakes for variety.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping a single guppy or only a pair, when the fish do best in a group of at least three.
- Keeping a 1:1 ratio of males to females, which can wear out and harass the females.
- Letting fry accumulate without a plan, since adults will eat the young and the tank can quickly become overcrowded.
- Housing guppies with a betta, which may attack bright, long-finned fish it mistakes for rivals.
