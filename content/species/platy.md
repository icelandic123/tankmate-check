## Overview
The platy is a small livebearer from southern Mexico and Central America. Adults reach about 2.4 inches (6 cm). The most useful fact for a beginner is that platies are among the hardiest community fish, and they are easy to keep in a group of three or more. Females give birth to free-swimming young rather than laying eggs, so breeding happens readily once a pair is established. Platies come in many colors, including red, orange, blue, and mixed patterns.

They are peaceful, active fish that spend time in the middle of the tank and get along with most calm community species. They are one of the best choices for a first tank, provided the water is kept stable.

## Tank setup
A group of three needs at least 15 gallons (57 L). Keep the water at 68–79 °F (20–26 °C) and the pH between 7.0 and 8.2, since platies prefer harder, slightly alkaline water. Plant the tank densely with hardy plants, and provide floating plants or other cover so fry have places to hide. A gentle filter with a sponge intake keeps young fish from being pulled in. Gravel or sand both work for the bottom.

## Feeding
Platies are omnivores that do well on a quality flake or pellet. Add vegetable matter such as blanched spinach or spirulina flakes, plus occasional brine shrimp or bloodworms. Feed once or twice a day, only what they eat in about two minutes, and remove anything left over.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping a single platy, when they do best in groups of three or more.
- Letting females breed without a plan for the fry, which can crowd a small tank quickly.
- Keeping them in soft, acidic water, when they do best in harder, alkaline water.
- Overfeeding, which pollutes the water in a small tank.
