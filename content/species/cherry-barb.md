## Overview
The cherry barb is a calm, colorful schooling fish from Sri Lanka. Adult males turn a rich red, while females are paler and slightly rounder. It is a good fit for a community tank and a welcome change from the more boisterous barbs.

Cherry barbs are peaceful and do best in a group of at least six. They swim in the middle of the tank and are easy to observe. Keep in mind that this barb is not the same as the tiger barb, which is a fin nipper and a poor tankmate for it.

## Tank setup
A group needs a tank of at least 15 gallons (57 L). Keep the water between 73 and 81 °F (23–27 °C) with a pH of 6.0 to 7.5. Plants along the sides and back, with open swimming space in the middle, suit them well. Gentle to moderate flow is best, and standard lighting works. Any substrate is fine, so choose one that makes the tank easy to maintain.

## Feeding
Cherry barbs are omnivores. Offer a quality flake or small pellet once or twice a day in amounts they finish within a few minutes. Add frozen or live foods like brine shrimp or bloodworms a few times a week to keep the fish colorful and healthy.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six, which leaves them shy and less colorful.
- Adding tiger barbs, which nip fins and are not calm companions for cherry barbs.
- Fitting a group into a tank smaller than 15 gallons, which crowds the school.
- Overfeeding, which fouls the water and stresses a small group of fish.
