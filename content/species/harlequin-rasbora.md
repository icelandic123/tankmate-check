## Overview
The harlequin rasbora is a small schooling fish from Southeast Asia, where it lives in slow, tea-colored streams and swamp waters. The most useful fact for a beginner is that it is a calm, peaceful fish that should be kept in a group of at least eight. In a group it shows its copper-orange body and the dark wedge-shaped marking on its side. Adults reach about 2 inches (5 cm).

Harlequin rasboras move together as a tight shoal rather than scattering around the tank. They are easy to keep and suit most community tanks, especially when they can see each other and feel safe in a planted setup.

## Tank setup
A group of eight needs at least 15 gallons (57 L). Plant the back and sides densely, leaving open swimming space in the middle, and add some driftwood or leaf litter to suggest the streams they come from. Sand or fine gravel works well on the bottom, and a gentle filter with moderate flow suits them. Keep the water at 72–81 °F (22–27 °C) and the pH between 6.0 and 7.5.

## Feeding
Harlequin rasboras are omnivores. Offer a quality flake or micro pellet every day, and add small frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, or bloodworms a few times a week. Their mouths are small, so choose foods they can take easily. Feed once or twice a day, in amounts they finish within a couple of minutes.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping only two or three, which leaves them skittish and less colorful.
- Pairing them with boisterous fish that dart through the school and keep it stressed.
- Using bright, open lighting with no cover, which makes the group nervous.
- Making sudden changes to water temperature or chemistry during water changes.
