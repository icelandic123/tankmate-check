## Overview
The black skirt tetra is a hardy, silvery schooling fish from South America. It is named for the dark patch along the rear part of its body and fins, which looks like a skirt. It is a good choice for a community tank with a beginner's level of experience.

These fish grow to about 2.4 inches (6 cm) and are most comfortable in a group of at least six. They swim actively in the middle of the tank. Keep in mind that they have a reputation for nipping fins, especially when kept in a small group.

## Tank setup
A proper group needs a tank of at least 20 gallons (76 L). Keep the water between 70 and 81 °F (21–27 °C) with a pH of 6.0 to 7.5. Leave open swimming space in the middle of the tank and place plants along the back and sides. Sturdy plants hold up well to their activity. Moderate flow and standard lighting suit them, and any substrate works.

## Feeding
Black skirt tetras are omnivores. Feed a quality flake or small pellet once or twice daily in portions they finish within a few minutes. Offer frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp or bloodworms a few times a week for variety.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six of them, which encourages fin nipping among the group and toward other fish.
- Pairing them with long-finned tankmates such as angelfish or bettas, which are common targets for nipping.
- Fitting a group into a tank smaller than 20 gallons, which leaves them crowded and stressed.
- Assuming they are immune to nipping problems because they are hardy, when a larger group is the main fix.
