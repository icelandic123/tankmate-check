## Overview
Rainbow sharks are territorial toward bottom dwellers and their own kind, so keep only one. They are bold bottom fish from the rivers of Thailand, growing to a typical adult length of about 6 in (15 cm). Despite the name, they are not sharks, though their tall dorsal fin gives them a shark-like look.

Given enough space, a lone rainbow shark is active and interesting to watch as it patrols the bottom. It does best with other fish that stay in the upper and middle water.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 55 gallons (208 L), since territorial fish need room to be left alone. Use a long tank with a sand or fine-gravel bottom, plus rocks, driftwood, and plants that break up the floor into distinct areas. Leave open swimming room in the upper and middle water. Keep the water at 75–81 °F (24–27 °C) with a pH between 6.5 and 7.5. Moderate lighting and moderate flow suit them.

## Feeding
Rainbow sharks are omnivores that feed mostly on the bottom. Sinking pellets or wafers make a good daily base, and blanched vegetables such as zucchini add variety. Add frozen or live foods like bloodworms or brine shrimp a few times a week. Feed in amounts that are cleared quickly, since uneaten food can foul the substrate.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping two or more, which leads to chasing and fighting over territory.
- Adding other bottom-dwelling fish that look similar, which the shark will chase.
- Using a tank smaller than 55 gallons (208 L) for an adult of this size.
- Filling the tank with open floor and no hiding spots, which leaves the shark stressed and more territorial.
