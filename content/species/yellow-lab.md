## Overview
Yellow lab cichlids need hard, alkaline water and belong with other African rift-lake cichlids, not with soft-water fish. They are bright, mild cichlids from Lake Malawi in East Africa, growing to a typical adult length of about 4 in (10 cm). Males are a vivid yellow, while females are often duller.

This is the mildest of the mbuna, the rock-dwelling cichlids of Lake Malawi, but it still holds a small territory and grazes algae from the rocks. Give it enough hiding spots that each fish can stay out of sight of the others.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 55 gallons (208 L), with a rocky layout that gives caves and territories. Stack stones to form caves, add a sand bottom for digging, and skip delicate plants, since mbuna graze and dig them up. Use strong filtration and steady water changes to keep the water clean and hard. Keep the water at 75–81 °F (24–27 °C) with a pH between 7.6 and 8.6. Do not add soft-water or acidic-water tankmates.

## Feeding
Yellow labs are omnivores that graze algae from rocks, so algae on the rockwork supplies part of their diet. Offer a high-quality cichlid pellet with plant matter such as spirulina once or twice a day, and add frozen or live foods a few times a week. Blanched vegetables like zucchini are useful. Feed only what they finish quickly to keep the water clean.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing them with soft-water fish from other regions, which need very different conditions.
- Using soft or acidic water, which causes stress and poor health in a rift-lake cichlid.
- Keeping them in a tank smaller than 55 gallons (208 L).
- Leaving the tank bare of rockwork, which makes them more aggressive toward each other.
