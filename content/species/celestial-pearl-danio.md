## Overview
The celestial pearl danio is a tiny schooling fish from a small region of Myanmar, and it was first described by scientists in 2007. Its most useful trait for a beginner is that it needs cooler water than most tropical community fish. Adults reach about 1 in (2.5 cm), with a body covered in teal and gold speckling, and males are the more brightly colored of the two sexes.

These fish are shy and do best in a group of six or more, where they feel secure enough to show their color. Keep them with other calm, small fish, since boisterous tankmates can overwhelm them and keep them hidden.

## Tank setup
A 10 gallon (38 L) tank is the minimum for a group of six, and a larger tank gives the school more room to settle. Keep the water at 68–77 °F (20–25 °C) with a pH of 6.5–7.5. Dense plants, floating cover, and moderate lighting suit them, and a gentle flow keeps the water well oxygenated without pushing the fish around. Use a lid, since small danios can jump.

## Feeding
Celestial pearl danios are omnivores that feed mostly in the upper and middle parts of the tank. Offer a fine flake or micro pellet daily, and add frozen or live foods such as baby brine shrimp, daphnia, or microworms a few times a week. Keep portions small, since leftover food breaks down quickly in a small tank.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping the water warmer than about 77 °F (25 °C) for long periods, which goes against their cooler preference.
- Keeping only a pair or trio, which leaves the fish shy and hidden.
- Pairing them with boisterous or fast-moving fish that will crowd and stress them.
- Setting up a bare, brightly lit tank with no cover, so the fish never settle in.
