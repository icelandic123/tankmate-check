## Overview
Keep neon tetras in a group of at least six, and only in a tank that has finished cycling, since they are sensitive to swings in water quality. They are small schooling fish from the upper Amazon basin of South America, growing to a typical adult length of about 1.4 in (3.5 cm). Their electric blue stripe and red lower body show best when they feel secure in a group.

They are peaceful midwater swimmers that get along with most calm fish of similar size. They do best in a tank that holds steady conditions over time.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 10 gallons (38 L) for a group of six. Set up a planted tank with live or silk plants along the back and sides, and leave open swimming room through the middle. Dark sand or fine gravel and a little driftwood or leaf litter help the fish feel at home and bring out their color. Keep the lighting subdued, and use gentle to moderate flow. Keep the water at 68–79 °F (20–26 °C) with a pH between 6.0 and 7.0.

## Feeding
Neon tetras are omnivores. Feed a high-quality flake or micro pellet made for small tropical fish once or twice a day, giving only what they finish within a few minutes. Offer frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, or bloodworms a few times a week for color and variety. They feed mostly in the upper and middle water, so scatter food across the surface to let the whole school get a share.

## Common mistakes
- Adding them to a tank that has not finished cycling, where ammonia or nitrite spikes can kill a whole group quickly.
- Buying only two or three, which leaves the fish stressed and shy.
- Keeping the lights bright and the tank bare, which washes out their color and makes them hide.
- Overfeeding, which fouls the water that these fish are so sensitive to.
