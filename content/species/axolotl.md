## Overview
The axolotl is an aquatic salamander from a small number of lake and canal systems around Mexico City. Adults reach about 10 inches (25 cm). The most useful fact for a beginner is that axolotls need cold water, 61–68 °F (16–20 °C), which is cooler than most tank setups. They also must be kept with care around other animals, because fish can nip at their feathery external gills. Adults keep their gills and stay in the water for life.

Axolotls are carnivores and will eat anything small enough to fit in their mouths. They are usually kept alone or with other axolotls of a similar size.

## Tank setup
A single adult needs at least 20 gallons (76 L). Keep the water at 61–68 °F (16–20 °C), and use a chiller or a cool room if the house runs warm. The pH should sit between 7.0 and 8.0. Use a gentle filter with low flow, since axolotls struggle in strong current. Use fine sand or a bare bottom, because axolotls swallow gravel while feeding. Provide hides and a few sturdy, cold-tolerant plants.

## Feeding
Axolotls are carnivores. Offer sinking pellets formulated for axolotls or carnivorous fish, along with earthworms, bloodworms, or small pieces of shrimp or krill as treats. Feed adults a few times a week. Remove any uneaten food promptly so it does not foul the cold water.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them in water above 68 °F (20 °C) for long periods.
- Housing them with fish that nip at their gills or are small enough to be eaten.
- Using gravel small enough to swallow, which can cause blockages.
- Handling them roughly or lifting them out of the water.
