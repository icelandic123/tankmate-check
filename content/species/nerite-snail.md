## Overview
Nerite snails are excellent algae eaters that do not overpopulate a freshwater tank, which makes them one of the most practical snails to keep. They are small freshwater snails from tropical waters, growing to a typical adult length of about 1 in (2.5 cm). Their shells come in striking patterns of dark and light markings.

They are slow, quiet, and peaceful. They graze along the glass, plants, and decor, and they get along well with calm fish.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 5 gallons (19 L), though a larger tank gives more algae to graze. Use a tight-fitting lid, since snails can climb out. Provide plants and smooth decor as grazing surfaces, and sand or fine gravel for the floor. Keep the water at 72–82 °F (22–28 °C) with a pH between 7.0 and 8.5. Moderate flow and standard lighting suit them well.

## Feeding
Nerite snails are herbivores that graze algae from glass, decor, and plants. A healthy algae supply covers most of their needs. When algae runs low, offer blanched vegetables such as zucchini or spinach, or algae wafers a few times a week. Remove uneaten vegetables promptly so they do not foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming a nerite will survive in a tank with no algae and no supplements.
- Leaving the lid off, so the snail can climb out and dry out.
- Scraping off the hard white eggs and treating them as a problem, when they do not hatch in fresh water and are harmless.
- Using copper-based medications, which are toxic to snails and shrimp.
