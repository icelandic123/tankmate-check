## Overview
The swordtail is a hardy, colorful livebearer from Central America. Males have a long, sword-like extension on the lower tail fin, which is where the fish gets its name. Females are larger and lack the sword. The swordtail is an easy, rewarding fish for a beginner who has room for a bigger tank.

Like guppies, swordtails give birth to live young, and they breed readily. They are peaceful, active fish that swim mostly in the middle of the tank. A small group with a balanced ratio of males and females keeps the community calm.

## Tank setup
A swordtail group needs a tank of at least 30 gallons (114 L). Keep the water between 72 and 82 °F (22–28 °C) with a pH of 7.0 to 8.4, which is slightly alkaline. Plant the tank with sturdy plants along the back and sides, and leave open swimming space in the middle. Moderate flow and standard lighting suit them well. Any substrate works.

## Feeding
Swordtails are omnivores. Offer a quality flake or pellet once or twice a day in portions they finish quickly. Include some plant matter such as spirulina flakes or blanched vegetables, and add frozen or live foods a few times a week.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping exactly two males, which lets one bully the other. Keep one male, or three or more so the sparring spreads out.
- Keeping males without enough females, which can leave the females harassed.
- Fitting a group into a tank smaller than 30 gallons, which leaves no room for the swimmers.
- Forgetting that females breed often, so the tank can fill with fry faster than expected.
