## Overview
The honey gourami is a small, gentle labyrinth fish from northeastern India and Bangladesh, where it lives in slow, heavily vegetated waters. Adults reach about 2 inches (5 cm). The most useful fact for a beginner is that it is the gentlest of the common gouramis, which makes it a good centerpiece for a small community tank. It has a golden-brown body, and males are the more colorful sex.

Like other labyrinth fish, it can gulp air from the surface. It is shy around boisterous fish, so it does best in a planted tank with dim lighting and quiet tankmates.

## Tank setup
A single fish or a pair fits in a tank of at least 10 gallons (38 L). Keep the water at 72–82 °F (22–28 °C) and the pH between 6.0 and 7.5. Plant the tank heavily, with some floating plants near the top and open swimming space in the middle. A gentle filter with low flow suits them, and sand or fine gravel works well on the bottom. A lid keeps the air just above the water warm and humid, which matters for a fish that breathes from the surface.

## Feeding
Honey gouramis are omnivores. Offer a quality flake or micro pellet as the base of the diet, and add frozen or live foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, or daphnia a few times a week. Vegetable matter such as blanched spinach or spirulina flakes rounds out the diet. Feed once or twice a day, only what the fish finish within a couple of minutes.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them in a tank smaller than 10 gallons (38 L), where water quality swings more quickly.
- Housing them with boisterous fish that outcompete them at feeding time.
- Letting the water drop below 72 °F (22 °C), which is the bottom of their comfortable range.
- Keeping them in very bright, open tanks with no plants or cover, which makes them shy.
