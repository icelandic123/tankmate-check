## Overview
The cherry shrimp is a small freshwater shrimp, usually bred in bright red and related colors. It is native to parts of East Asia and grows to about 1.2 inches (3 cm). The most useful fact for a beginner is that cherry shrimp should be kept in a group of at least ten. They breed readily in a planted tank, so a small group can become a colony over time. Adults are peaceful and spend most of their time on the bottom and on plants.

They graze continuously on algae, biofilm, and soft plant matter. Their young are eaten by almost any fish, so the tank usually works best as a shrimp tank or with fish that will not prey on tiny shrimp.

## Tank setup
A group of ten needs at least 5 gallons (19 L). Plant the tank densely with fine-leaved and mossy plants, which give shrimp grazing surfaces and hiding spots for young. Keep the water at 64–82 °F (18–28 °C) and the pH between 6.5 and 8.0. Use a sponge filter or a gentle filter with a covered intake, since young shrimp can be pulled into an open intake. A mature tank with stable water and a fine substrate suits them well.

## Feeding
Cherry shrimp are omnivores that graze on biofilm, algae, and soft plant matter throughout the day. An established, planted tank supplies much of this. Offer a shrimp-specific pellet, blanched vegetables such as spinach or zucchini, or algae wafers a few times a week. Feed small amounts and remove uneaten vegetables before they rot.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than ten, which makes breeding and feeding less reliable.
- Using a filter with an open intake, which can pull in and kill young shrimp.
- Using medications that contain copper, which are toxic to shrimp.
- Housing them with fish that will eat the babies, which removes the colony's young.
