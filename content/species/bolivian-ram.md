## Overview
The Bolivian ram is a dwarf cichlid from South America. It is a colorful, bottom-dwelling fish that digs and explores the substrate. It is one of the more forgiving dwarf cichlids and a good choice for a beginner who wants a cichlid with personality.

Bolivian rams are peaceful with most fish but territorial, especially when they are settling in or breeding. Expect a pair to claim a patch of the bottom and defend it. They grow to about 3.1 inches (8 cm) and stay near the bottom and mid levels of the tank.

## Tank setup
A tank of at least 30 gallons (114 L) gives them room to claim territory. Keep the water between 72 and 79 °F (22–26 °C) with a pH of 6.0 to 7.5. Use a sand or fine gravel substrate, add caves and flat stones for shelter, and leave open floor space for digging. Plants can be added along the back and sides. Gentle flow and standard lighting suit them well.

## Feeding
Bolivian rams are omnivores. Offer sinking pellets, frozen foods such as bloodworms or brine shrimp, and occasional vegetable matter. Feed once or twice a day in small portions that are finished within a few minutes.

## Common mistakes
- Housing them in a tank smaller than 30 gallons, which leaves no room for territories.
- Adding dwarf shrimp or very small fish, which they will eat.
- Keeping two males in a tank without enough caves and space, which leads to fights.
- Treating them as identical to the German blue ram, when the Bolivian ram tolerates a wider range of water conditions.
