## Overview
The common pleco is a large armored catfish from the rivers of South America. It is often sold as a small algae eater, but it grows to 45 cm (about 18 inches) or more. The most useful fact for a beginner is to plan for the adult size from the start, because a fish that looks small in the store will outgrow most home aquariums. Juveniles are small and easy to overlook, while adults have bony plates along the body and a wide, sucker-like mouth.

In the wild it grazes on algae and feeds on wood, and it is most active at night. In a home aquarium it is a calm, peaceful fish that does best on its own in a very large, well-kept tank.

## Tank setup
An adult needs a very large tank, at least 151 gallons (570 L). Include driftwood, since the fish needs fiber in its diet, along with caves large enough for an adult to hide in. Use smooth sand or fine, smooth gravel, and a strong filter that handles the waste of a large fish. Keep the water at 72–82 °F (22–28 °C) and the pH between 6.5 and 7.8. Plants may need to be sturdy or artificial, since adults graze and rasp at surfaces.

## Feeding
Common plecos are herbivores. Offer vegetables such as blanched zucchini, cucumber, or leafy greens, along with sinking algae wafers or pellets made for plecos. Driftwood in the tank gives them fiber to graze on. Feed mostly in the evening or after the lights go off, when they are most active, and remove uneaten vegetables before they foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Buying a small juvenile as an algae eater without planning for an adult size of 45 cm (about 18 inches).
- Keeping it with slow, flat-sided fish that its rasping can damage.
- Relying on algae in the tank alone and offering no vegetables.
- Keeping it without driftwood, which leaves its diet short of fiber.
