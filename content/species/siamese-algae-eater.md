## Overview
Young Siamese algae eaters graze algae most readily, so buy a juvenile if the fish's main job is algae control. They are slender, silver-gray bottom fish from the rivers of Southeast Asia, growing to a typical adult length of about 6 in (15 cm). That is much larger than many fish sold under the "algae eater" name.

Adults can become pushy with fish that look similar, and they need room to swim along the bottom. They are generally peaceful with other fish in a community tank that has enough space.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 30 gallons (114 L) to give an adult room to move. Use a long tank with a smooth floor of sand or fine gravel, plus rocks, driftwood, and sturdy plants. Moderate flow keeps the water oxygenated. Lighting can be moderate, since steady algae growth supports the fish. Keep the water at 75–81 °F (24–27 °C) with a pH between 6.5 and 8.0.

## Feeding
Siamese algae eaters are omnivores. Young fish graze algae well, and a tank with steady algae growth can meet much of their needs. Adults often need extra food, so offer sinking wafers or pellets, plus blanched vegetables such as zucchini a few times a week. Feed on the bottom, and remove leftovers before they foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Buying an adult expecting it to clear a new tank of algae, when young fish are far better grazers.
- Keeping it in a tank smaller than 30 gallons (114 L).
- Adding fish that look similar to it, which the adult may chase.
- Relying on algae alone and offering no supplementary food once the algae runs low.
