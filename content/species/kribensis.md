## Overview
Kribensis are peaceful most of the time, but a breeding pair defends its cave hard, so give them a cave and room to be left alone. They are colorful dwarf cichlids from the lowland rivers of West Africa, in Nigeria and Cameroon, growing to a typical adult length of about 4 in (10 cm). For a beginner, a single pair with a cave is the simplest way to keep them.

They usually stay near the bottom. Females develop a bright red or purple belly when ready to spawn, and the pair works together to guard its eggs and young.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 30 gallons (114 L) for a pair with other fish. Provide a cave or a flowerpot-style shelter for spawning, and use sand or fine gravel on the floor. Add plants, driftwood, and open floor space in front of the cave. Moderate flow suits them. Keep the water at 75–81 °F (24–27 °C) with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5, and avoid other bottom dwellers that compete for the cave.

## Feeding
Kribensis are omnivores. Offer sinking pellets or flakes once or twice a day, and add frozen or live foods such as bloodworms or brine shrimp a few times a week. Vegetable matter like blanched spinach or zucchini adds variety. Feed near the bottom, where they forage, and remove leftovers that could foul the substrate.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping several males together, since they will fight over territory.
- Ignoring the cave, which a pair needs to feel secure and to spawn.
- Housing them with fish small enough to eat, since they can take prey up to about 0.8 in (2 cm).
- Assuming a peaceful temperament means they will tolerate any bottom-dwelling neighbor during spawning.
