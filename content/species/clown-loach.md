## Overview
Clown loaches need a very large tank and a group of at least five, because they grow far larger than most buyers expect. They are striking, social bottom fish from Sumatra and Borneo in Southeast Asia, with an orange body and bold black bands. Adults reach a typical length of about 12 in (30 cm), so a small fish at the store will not stay small.

They are peaceful and active, and they are happiest when they can see other clown loaches. Much of their time is spent exploring the floor and the decor.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 125 gallons (475 L) for a group of five, since adults get large. Use a long tank with plenty of open floor space, sand or smooth gravel, and caves or hiding places made from rock, driftwood, or pipe. Add a secure lid, because clown loaches are known to escape. Keep the water at 77–86 °F (25–30 °C) with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5, and use moderate flow with good oxygenation.

## Feeding
Clown loaches are omnivores that accept most sinking foods. Offer sinking pellets or wafers daily, and add frozen or live foods such as bloodworms or brine shrimp a few times a week. Blanched vegetables and algae-based foods provide useful variety. Feed near the bottom, where they forage, and do not leave uneaten food to rot in the tank.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than five, which leaves a lone fish stressed and shy.
- Buying them for a tank far too small for their adult size.
- Housing them with fish or shrimp small enough to eat, since they will take prey up to about 0.8 in (2 cm).
- Assuming snails in the tank are safe, because clown loaches eat them eagerly.
