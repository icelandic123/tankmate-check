## Overview
Tiger barbs are fin nippers, so keep at least six together to spread their energy across the group instead of their tankmates. They are lively, striped schooling fish from Southeast Asia, around Sumatra and Borneo, growing to a typical adult length of about 2.8 in (7 cm). Their gold body and black bars make them one of the best-known community fish.

They are active and boisterous, and they do best in a spacious tank with room to chase each other. They should not share a tank with slow, long-finned fish.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 30 gallons (114 L) for a group of six. Use a long tank with open swimming space, plus plants and hardy decor such as driftwood or rocks to break up sight lines. A dark substrate helps their coloring show. Moderate lighting and moderate flow suit them. Keep the water at 72–81 °F (22–27 °C) with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5, and avoid tankmates with long, slow fins.

## Feeding
Tiger barbs are omnivores that eat most standard foods. Offer a quality flake or pellet once or twice a day in small portions, and add frozen or live foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, or daphnia a few times a week. Blanched vegetables or algae-based foods round out the diet. They swim at several levels, so feed across the water column.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than six, which causes the group to focus its nipping on other fish.
- Housing them with long-finned fish such as bettas, angelfish, or fancy goldfish.
- Using a tank smaller than 30 gallons (114 L) for a proper group.
- Keeping them in a bare tank with no plants or cover, which leaves them stressed and more aggressive.
