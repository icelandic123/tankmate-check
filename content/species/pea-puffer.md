## Overview
The pea puffer is a tiny puffer from southern India, where it lives in slow, heavily vegetated fresh water. Adults reach about 1 in (2.5 cm). Pea puffers are best kept alone or in a species-only tank, since they are persistent fin nippers and hunters that will harass tankmates of almost any kind.

Each puffer has its own curious personality, and many keepers enjoy watching them explore and hunt. Because they are so small and so active in their own way, a single pea puffer in a well-planted tank is usually a more satisfying setup than a crowded community.

## Tank setup
A 5 gallon (19 L) tank is the minimum for a single pea puffer, and a larger tank gives more room to explore. Keep the water at 75–82 °F (24–28 °C) with a pH of 7.0–8.0. Use dense plants and hiding places, and include a lid, since puffers are active explorers. Avoid shrimp and small fish, because they will be hunted.

## Feeding
Pea puffers are carnivores. Offer live or frozen foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, daphnia, and snails. Feed a variety of small foods several times a week, and provide snails regularly, since shell-cracking helps keep the teeth worn down. Remove any uneaten live food promptly so it does not foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Housing them with shrimp or small fish, which they will hunt.
- Mixing them with calm community fish, which they will nip and chase.
- Feeding only soft foods and no snails, which leaves the teeth without the wear they need.
- Leaving the tank uncovered, since these active puffers can climb and escape.
