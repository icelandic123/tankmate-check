## Overview
The German blue ram is a dwarf cichlid from the Orinoco basin in Venezuela and Colombia. Adults reach about 2.4 inches (6 cm). The most useful fact for a beginner is that it needs warm, soft, clean water, which makes it a demanding fish compared with most dwarf cichlids. Males show an iridescent blue body with gold markings and longer fins, and pairs form a strong bond.

Pairs claim a patch of the bottom as their territory and defend it, especially when they have eggs or fry. Outside of breeding they are gentle and can live with calm, peaceful fish of similar size.

## Tank setup
A single ram or a pair fits in a tank of at least 20 gallons (76 L). Keep the water at 81–86 °F (27–30 °C), with a pH between 5.0 and 7.0 and soft water. Fine sand suits their bottom-sifting habit, and flat stones or caves give them places to spawn and shelter. Plant the back of the tank with sturdy plants, and use a gentle filter. Frequent partial water changes with clean, soft water help keep conditions stable.

## Feeding
German blue rams are omnivores with a strong interest in small live and frozen foods. Offer quality sinking wafers or pellets along with bloodworms, brine shrimp, or daphnia a few times a week. Feed small portions once or twice a day, since their mouths are small. Remove leftovers promptly so they do not foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them in cool or hard water, which stresses them over time.
- Keeping more than one pair in a small tank, which leads to territorial fights.
- Changing water temperature or chemistry quickly when acclimating or during water changes.
- Housing them with large, boisterous fish that crowd them out of their territory.
