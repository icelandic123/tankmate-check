## Overview
The white cloud mountain minnow is a small, slender schooling fish from the mountain streams of southern China, where the water stays cool. The most useful fact for a beginner is that it is a cool-water fish and does not need a heater in most homes, which makes it one of the easiest fish to keep. Adults reach about 1.6 inches (4 cm). They have a silver body with a dark stripe along the side and reddish fins, and they show their best color in a group of six or more.

They are active and peaceful and spend most of their time in the upper and middle parts of the tank. They suit community tanks with other calm, cool-tolerant fish.

## Tank setup
A group of six needs at least 10 gallons (38 L). Keep the water between 57 and 72 °F (14–22 °C), which is typical of a cool room, and check it with a thermometer. A heater is usually not needed, but a tropical setup running warmer than this range is not suitable. Plant the tank with hardy plants and leave open swimming space near the top. A gentle filter with low to moderate flow and a fine sand or gravel bottom suit them well, with a pH between 6.0 and 8.0.

## Feeding
White cloud mountain minnows are omnivores. A quality flake or small pellet makes a good everyday staple, with occasional small frozen or live foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia. Feed once or twice a day in small amounts that the group eats within a couple of minutes. Because they feed mostly near the surface, floating food works well.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them in a warm tropical tank, around 80 °F (27 °C), which is well above their cool-water range.
- Keeping only one or two, which leaves them shy and less active.
- Housing them with large or fast fish that harass them at feeding time.
- Overfeeding, which quickly clouds the water in a small tank.
