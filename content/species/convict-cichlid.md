## Overview
The convict cichlid is a hardy, striped cichlid from Central America. Its black-and-cream vertical bars are easy to recognize. It is a popular fish for beginners who want a cichlid that is simple to keep, but it is a much more aggressive fish than its size suggests.

Convict cichlids are small, but they are extremely territorial, especially when breeding, which they do readily. They are best kept as a single pair in a species tank or with other robust cichlids. Expect them to defend a spot on the bottom vigorously.

## Tank setup
A single pair needs a tank of at least 30 gallons (114 L). Keep the water between 68 and 82 °F (20–28 °C) with a pH of 6.5 to 8.0. Provide caves, rocks, and flat stones for shelter, and leave some open floor for digging. Sand or gravel works as a substrate, and plants should be sturdy or artificial, since they are often uprooted. Use strong filtration and a secure lid.

## Feeding
Convict cichlids are omnivores. Offer quality pellets, flakes, and frozen foods such as bloodworms, with some vegetable matter included. Feed once or twice a day in moderate portions, since these cichlids produce a fair amount of waste.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them with peaceful community fish, which will be harassed or killed.
- Choosing a tank smaller than 30 gallons, which leaves no room for a breeding pair to hold its ground.
- Adding dwarf shrimp or other small fish, which they will eat.
- Overfeeding, which adds waste and stresses the pair.
