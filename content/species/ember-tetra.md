## Overview
The ember tetra is a tiny fish from Brazil that grows to about 2 cm (under 1 inch). The most useful fact for a beginner is that it is a shoaling fish and should be kept in a group of at least eight. In a group it feels secure and shows more of its glowing orange-red color. It is peaceful and timid, so calm tankmates that will not crowd it work best.

In the wild it lives in slow, soft, slightly acidic streams and forest waters where leaf litter and plants are common. In an aquarium it does best in a planted tank with subdued lighting, where it can move between the plants and the open water.

## Tank setup
Keep a group of eight in a tank of at least 10 gallons (38 L). Plant the tank densely with fine-leaved plants, add driftwood or leaf litter for a natural look, and keep the lighting dim or partly shaded. A fine sand or small-grain substrate suits them, along with a gentle filter, since they struggle in strong current. Keep the water at 73–84 °F (23–29 °C) and the pH between 5.5 and 7.0.

## Feeding
Ember tetras are omnivores. Offer a high-quality micro flake or micro pellet as a staple, and add small live or frozen foods such as baby brine shrimp, daphnia, or microworms. Their mouths are very small, so food must be finely sized. Feed once or twice a day, only what they eat within a minute or two, and remove any leftovers.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping fewer than eight, which leaves them shy and stressed.
- Pairing them with large or boisterous fish that hide them away and keep them from feeding.
- Using bright, open lighting with no plants or cover, which leaves them pale and nervous.
- Offering food that is too large for their small mouths.
