## Overview
The oscar is a large cichlid from the Amazon and Orinoco basins in South America. Adults reach about 13 inches (33 cm). The most useful fact for a beginner is that an oscar is a carnivore that will eat any fish it can swallow, and it produces a large amount of waste. Its tank and tankmates must be planned around that from the start. Oscars have a bold, curious personality and often come to the front of the glass.

That same boldness makes them difficult tankmates. Most keepers house them alone or with other large, robust fish that can hold their own.

## Tank setup
A single adult needs at least 75 gallons (284 L). Use a long, sturdy tank with strong filtration, since oscars produce heavy waste and need frequent water changes. Sand or smooth gravel works well on the bottom. Use large, sturdy rocks or caves, and keep plants to a minimum, since large fish can uproot them. Keep the water at 72–81 °F (22–27 °C) and the pH between 6.0 and 8.0. A secure lid is recommended.

## Feeding
Oscars are carnivores. Offer a quality pellet designed for large carnivorous cichlids as the staple, and add frozen foods such as krill, shrimp, and bloodworms a few times a week. Feed once a day in an amount the fish finishes within a few minutes. Remove uneaten food so it does not add to the waste load.

## Common mistakes
- Housing them with small fish, which they will eat once the fish fits in their mouths.
- Underestimating the filtration and water changes needed for their heavy waste output.
- Keeping an adult in a tank much smaller than its adult size of about 13 inches (33 cm).
- Feeding live feeder fish, which can introduce disease into the tank.
