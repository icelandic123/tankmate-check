## Overview
Otocinclus do best in a mature tank with established algae and biofilm, so avoid adding them to a new tank. They are small, slender catfish from tropical South America, growing to a typical adult length of about 1.6 in (4 cm), with a dark horizontal stripe along the side. Because they are often sold young and struggle in a sterile tank, buy them only when the tank has been running for a while.

They are peaceful shoaling fish that do best in a group of at least six. They spend their days grazing on plants, glass, and decorations, and they are generally not bothered by calm tankmates.

## Tank setup
Plan on at least 15 gallons (57 L) for a group of six. Set up a well-planted, mature tank with broad-leaved plants and smooth decor where biofilm can grow. Use a smooth substrate such as sand or fine gravel, with moderate flow and good oxygenation. Keep the water at 70–81 °F (21–27 °C) with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5. A lid is optional. Avoid heavy chemical treatments that would strip the algae they depend on.

## Feeding
Otocinclus are herbivores. In a mature tank they graze biofilm and soft algae for much of the day. Because a tank may not supply enough food, offer blanched vegetables such as zucchini or cucumber, and algae-based sinking foods a few times a week. Keep portions small, and remove uneaten vegetables before they foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Buying them for a new, uncycled tank that has no biofilm or algae to graze.
- Keeping only one or two, since these fish are shoaling and do best in a group.
- Relying on algae alone and offering no vegetable supplements.
- Cleaning the tank too thoroughly, which removes the biofilm they depend on.
