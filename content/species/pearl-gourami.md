## Overview
The pearl gourami is a calm, attractive labyrinth fish from Southeast Asia. Its body is covered in pearl-like spots, and adult males develop a bright red-orange chest during breeding condition. Like other gouramis, it can breathe air at the surface using a special organ, which lets it live in warm, shallow, slow-moving water in the wild.

Pearl gouramis are peaceful for their size and suit a beginner who wants a larger, gentle centerpiece fish. They usually stay in the upper and middle levels of the tank. Adult males can be quarrelsome with other gouramis, so keep only one.

## Tank setup
A single pearl gourami needs a tank of at least 30 gallons (114 L). Keep the water between 75 and 82 °F (24–28 °C) with a pH of 6.0 to 7.5. Provide plenty of plants, both rooted and floating, and leave open swimming space. Gentle to moderate flow is best, and a secure lid helps prevent jumping. Standard lighting and any substrate work well.

## Feeding
Pearl gouramis are omnivores. Offer a quality flake or pellet once or twice a day in portions they finish within a few minutes. Add frozen or live foods such as bloodworms or brine shrimp a few times a week, and include some vegetable matter.

## Common mistakes
- Adding dwarf shrimp or very small fish, which the gourami may eat.
- Keeping two adult males, which quarrel with each other and with other gouramis.
- Housing it in a tank smaller than 30 gallons, which leaves it cramped as an adult.
- Leaving the tank open on top, which gives an adult gourami an easy route to jump out.
