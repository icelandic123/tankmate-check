## Overview
The chili rasbora is one of the smallest fish in the aquarium hobby, reaching only about 0.8 inch (2 cm) as an adult. It comes from Southeast Asia, where it lives in shallow, soft, acidic waters. Its bright red color is striking in a small group, which makes it a rewarding fish for a beginner who wants a compact tank.

Chili rasbora are peaceful, shoaling fish that feel safest in a group of at least eight. Their small size is the main challenge. Bigger tankmates can eat them or push them away from food, so choose carefully.

## Tank setup
A group needs a tank of at least 5 gallons (19 L). Keep the water between 68 and 82 °F (20–28 °C) with a pH of 4.0 to 7.0, which is softer and more acidic than most community tanks. A densely planted setup with floating plants and dim lighting gives them cover and a calm environment. Use gentle flow, and choose a fine substrate that is easy to keep clean.

## Feeding
Chili rasbora are omnivores that eat small prey. Feed a high-quality micro pellet or finely crushed flake, along with baby brine shrimp or daphnia when available. Keep portions small and remove leftovers so the water stays clean.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them with larger fish such as cichlids or big tetras, which can eat them or outcompete them at feeding time.
- Keeping fewer than eight, which leaves a solitary fish stressed and shy.
- Using hard, alkaline water with a pH above 7.0, which does not suit their natural habitat.
- Setting up a bare tank with no plants or cover, which leaves them feeling exposed.
