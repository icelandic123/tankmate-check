## Overview
Discus are large, disc-shaped cichlids from the Amazon basin of South America. In the wild they live in warm, soft, acidic water, and they are among the most demanding fish in the freshwater hobby. They are not recommended for a first tank. Adults reach about 7 in (18 cm), and they are best appreciated as a slow, graceful group that shows its best color when relaxed.

Discus are peaceful and do best in a group of five or more, where they form a loose social structure. Keep them with other discus rather than mixed fish, since few tankmates tolerate the high temperatures they need.

## Tank setup
A 75 gallon (284 L) tank is the minimum for a group of five. Keep the water at 82–88 °F (28–31 °C) with a pH of 5.0–7.0, and use soft water with very low nitrate. A gentle filter, moderate flow, and frequent water changes are essential. Plant the tank with broad-leaved plants and provide shelter with wood or rock. A tight lid helps hold heat and humidity.

## Feeding
Discus are carnivores in the wild, and they need a diet rich in animal protein in captivity. Offer high-quality frozen or live foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, and mysis shrimp, along with a prepared food formulated for discus. Feed small meals two or three times a day, and remove uneaten food so it does not foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping them in a small tank or alone, when they need a group of five and plenty of space.
- Letting nitrate climb between water changes, since discus are very sensitive to poor water quality.
- Using hard, alkaline tap water without adjustment, which stresses the fish and dulls their color.
- Stocking them with tankmates that cannot tolerate their high temperature requirements.
