## Overview
The betta, also called the Siamese fighting fish, is a small labyrinth fish from Southeast Asia, where it lives in shallow, slow-moving water such as rice paddies, ditches, and marsh edges. The most useful fact for a beginner is that males must be kept alone. Two males will fight, often to the death, so one male per tank is the standard approach. Males have long, flowing fins and bright color, while females are plainer and smaller. Adults reach about 2.5 inches (6.5 cm).

Bettas are semi-aggressive. They may flare at or attack bright, long-finned fish they mistake for rivals, so choose tankmates with care. They can gulp air at the surface, which is why they are often seen near the top of the tank.

## Tank setup
A single betta does well in a tank of at least 5 gallons (19 L). Provide a gentle filter with low flow, since bettas tire in strong current, and keep the water at 75–82 °F (24–28 °C) with a heater and a pH between 6.0 and 7.5. Broad-leaved plants and a few resting spots near the surface give the fish places to rest. Smooth gravel or sand works well on the bottom. Use a lid, because bettas can jump.

## Feeding
Bettas are carnivores and do best on a varied diet. Offer a high-quality betta pellet as the staple, and add frozen or live foods such as bloodworms, brine shrimp, or daphnia a few times a week. Feed small amounts once or twice a day, only as much as the fish can finish in about two minutes. Remove any uneaten food so it does not foul the water.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping two males in the same tank, or placing a male with other bright, long-finned fish.
- Using a bowl or tank below the minimum size with no heater, so the water stays too cool for the fish.
- Overfeeding, which fouls the water quickly and leaves excess food on the bottom.
- Leaving the tank uncovered, so the fish can jump out.
